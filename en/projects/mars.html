Mars landing rover

Our entry for the Mission Mars 2026 Rover Landing Competition. We wrote our own quadcopter flight controller on an ESP32 and added a camera that reads ground markers, so the craft finds its landing spot by itself.

Competition
Mission Mars 2026 Rover Landing Competition (Tunghai University, National Museum of Natural Science)
When
2026.10 – 2026.11
Result
Finished
Role
Flight controller, camera and mechanism design (a classmate made the parachute)
Tech
ESP32, XIAO ESP32S3 camera, MPU attitude sensing, BMP280 barometer, PID, 3D printing

Goal

Get the craft to find a marker on the ground by itself and land steadily.

What I built

  • Flight controller: our own quadcopter firmware on an ESP32, running attitude estimation and PID in a 200 Hz loop, with a BMP280 barometer for altitude.
  • Camera board: a XIAO ESP32S3 camera watches the ground and sends what it sees to the flight controller over UART; positioning uses AprilTag markers.
  • Parachute release and separation each run on their own servo, and a safety switch keeps the motors from starting by accident.
  • The frame is 3D printed. I designed the flight controller, the camera system and the mechanism; a classmate made the parachute.

What got in the way

  • The ESP32 toolchain can't handle Chinese folder names, so the build failed whenever the project sat in a Chinese-named folder. I moved the real files to an English path and linked the folder back.
  • The firmware must not crash when a sensor is missing, so I added fault detection: if a sensor can't be read, it retries automatically and reports its status.
